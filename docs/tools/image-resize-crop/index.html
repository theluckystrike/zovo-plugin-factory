Questions

Is my image uploaded anywhere?

No. The page reads the image inside this tab, and your browser decodes and encodes it. Its Content Security Policy sets connect-src to none, so the page cannot send your image anywhere. Open the Network tab of your browser tools and you will see no request when you open or save an image.

How do I crop to a ratio such as 16:9 or 1:1?

Pick the ratio in the Crop list: Free, Original, 1:1, 4:3, 3:4, 3:2, 2:3, 16:9, 9:16 or your own. The page puts the biggest box of that ratio in the middle. Drag the box to move it and its corners to change its size; the ratio stays. You can also type X, Y, W and H in pixels.

How does resizing keep the image sharp?

When you make an image smaller, the page halves it in steps and then scales it to the exact size, which keeps edges and text sharper than one big jump. Resize by pixels or by percent, keep the ratio or not, or use Fit in 1920, 1280 or 800 pixels.

Which format should I save as?

PNG keeps every pixel and transparency, and suits screenshots and logos. JPEG is small for photos; clear parts become white. WebP is often smaller than both. The page shows the size of the new file before you save, so you can try the Quality slider and compare.

Are photos from a phone the right way up?

Yes. The browser applies the EXIF orientation when it opens the photo, and the page shows the orientation value it found. The saved copy has pixels only, so EXIF data such as the camera model or the GPS position is not in it. The page tells you when the file you opened has a GPS position.

Which files can it open, and how big?

PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF and BMP. The type comes from the first bytes, not the name. A GIF opens at its first frame. Images up to 50 megapixels open here; the size in the header is checked before anything is decoded. TIFF, HEIC, AVIF and SVG are not opened.

Does it work offline?

Yes. After the first visit the page keeps a copy of its own files, so it works with no connection.
