Questions
Is my photo uploaded anywhere?
No. The page reads the file inside this tab. Its Content Security Policy sets connect-src to none, so it cannot send your photo anywhere, and it loads no library from another site. Open the Network tab of your browser tools and you will see no request when you open an image. The OpenStreetMap link next to a GPS position is a normal link that only opens if you click it.
What metadata does it show?
The type, pixel size and transparency from the file header, then the EXIF block: camera make and model, lens, exposure time, aperture, ISO, focal length, flash, the date taken with its time zone, orientation and software. The GPS part shows latitude, longitude and altitude in degrees and as decimal numbers. PNG text chunks (tEXt, zTXt, iTXt), JPEG and GIF comments, the ICC color profile name, XMP and the list of JPEG segments or PNG and WebP chunks are shown too.
Which files have EXIF?
Photos from phones and cameras store EXIF in JPEG and HEIC files, and many editors keep it in WebP, PNG (eXIf chunk) and TIFF. Screenshots, most PNG and GIF files, and images saved for the web often have none, and the page says so.
How do I remove GPS and EXIF from a photo?
Open a JPEG and click Save a copy without EXIF and GPS. The copy drops the EXIF, XMP and Photoshop IPTC parts and keeps the compressed image bytes as they are, so there is no quality loss. A phone photo may then show sideways in some apps, because its Orientation tag is part of EXIF. This button is for JPEG files only.
Why is there no preview for my HEIC or TIFF file?
Most browsers cannot draw HEIC and TIFF images. The page still reads their type, pixel size and EXIF from the file. The Image Preview extension does the same in VS Code: it measures these files from the header.
Is the MakerNote decoded?
No. Camera makers store extra data in a MakerNote in their own formats. The page shows its size only.
