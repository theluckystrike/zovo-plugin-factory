Competition

How products in the same marketplaces (VS Code Marketplace, Open VSX, JetBrains Marketplace) earn money, what we learned, and what we change. F means fetched from the vendor's own page; E means an estimate or our reading; U means unverified (third-party blogs).

Updated 2026-11-13 14:00 (UTC+7).

Biggest insights

  1. Rivals charge far more than us. SQLite Viewer (3.77 M installs) sells PRO at USD 99 once or USD 24 a month and Business at USD 159; Wallaby.js is about USD 150. Zovo Pro is USD 19 once for all viewers.
  2. Even very large free viewers sell few licences: SQLite Viewer's own page says 'Purchased 1 times last month'. Revenue comes from a few buyers paying a higher price, and from businesses.
  3. Rivals sell through a merchant of record (SQLite Viewer: Polar, payments by Stripe), so the checkout shows one clean brand and VAT is handled. Our Stripe page shows the company, 'Pay BeLikeNative' and Zovo Pro.
  4. A 14-day refund guarantee is normal for one-time viewer licences. We have none.
  5. Businesses pay, individuals use it free: Thunder Client made free use personal and non-commercial; GitLens charges for private repos; Wallaby sells company licences.
  6. Subscriptions dominate the bigger players (GitLens/GitKraken, Thunder Client, Console Ninja, DBCode); one-time sellers also offer a monthly option for companies.
  7. Other money: sponsored content inside the free extension with 'ad-free' as a PRO perk (SQLite Viewer); a free viewer as lead generation for a paid SDK (MESCIUS Excel Viewer, 'Powered by SpreadJS').
  8. The VS Code Marketplace has no built-in paid extensions; the JetBrains Marketplace sells, licenses and handles tax for paid plugins (example split 85/15).
