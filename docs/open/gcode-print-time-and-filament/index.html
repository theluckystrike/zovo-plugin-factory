Print time and filament use from a G-code file

GCode Viewer reads a .gcode file and shows the print time and the filament length and weight in its Stats panel, next to the 3D toolpath. The time is worked out from the feed rate of every move, plus dwells. When the slicer wrote its own estimate (Cura, PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer or Bambu Studio), it shows too. The weight uses the filament diameter and density from the settings. The viewer and the stats are free. Zovo Pro (USD 19 once) adds a job report per layer, a compare of two G-code files and a folder summary, with 3 free tries each.

Steps in VS Code

  1. Install GCode Viewer from the Marketplace, or press Ctrl+P (Cmd+P on macOS) and run ext install zovo.zovo-gcode-viewer.
  2. Click a .gcode or .gco file in the Explorer. For .nc, .g, .ngc, .tap and .cnc, right-click the file and pick "Open in GCode Viewer".
  3. Look at the Stats panel on the right. Print time is the estimate from feed rates. Slicer time is the slicer's own number, when the file has one.
  4. Filament shows the length in meters from the E axis (absolute or relative) and the weight in grams. When the slicer wrote its own filament use, it shows as Slicer filament.
  5. The weight uses gcodeViewer.filamentDiameter (1.75 mm) and gcodeViewer.filamentDensity (1.24 g/cm³ for PLA). For PETG set 1.27, for ABS 1.04.
  6. The same panel shows the size and X, Y and Z ranges of the part, the speed range, retractions and temperatures. Click Copy to copy the stats as text.

Steps in Cursor, Windsurf or VSCodium

  1. Open the Extensions view with Ctrl+Shift+X (Cmd+Shift+X on macOS).
  2. Search for zovo.zovo-gcode-viewer and click Install. Cursor, Windsurf and VSCodium install from Open VSX, where it is published as zovo/zovo-gcode-viewer.
  3. If your editor has no gallery, download the .vsix from the Open VSX page and run "Extensions: Install from VSIX...".
  4. Then use it the same way as in VS Code.

What GCode Viewer handles

  • Slicer estimates from Cura (;TIME:), PrusaSlicer, OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio.
  • Absolute and relative E values for the filament length.
  • Layers from slicer comments such as ;LAYER: or ;LAYER_CHANGE, or from Z heights when there are none.
  • CNC programs, with the cycle time, cut and rapid lengths, the tools and the spindle speed.

What it does not do

  • Time estimates use feed rates only, without acceleration, jerk or firmware limits, so real prints often take a little longer.
  • Binary G-code (.bgcode) is not read. Export plain text G-code from the slicer instead.
  • Files over 500 MB are not read (setting gcodeViewer.maxFileMB).
  • Lines are drawn 1 pixel wide, not as round extrusion tubes.

Other options

  • Your slicer's preview. PrusaSlicer, Cura, OrcaSlicer and Bambu Studio show their estimated time and filament use after slicing. Free, outside VS Code.
  • PrusaSlicer G-code Viewer. A free standalone viewer that comes with PrusaSlicer. It opens a .gcode file with a layer preview, outside VS Code.

Questions

Why is the time shorter than my slicer's estimate?

GCode Viewer uses the feed rate of each move and does not model acceleration, jerk or firmware limits. When the slicer wrote its own estimate into the file, it shows as Slicer time next to it.

How is the filament weight worked out?

From the filament length on the E axis, the filament diameter (gcodeViewer.filamentDiameter, 1.75 mm by default) and the density (gcodeViewer.filamentDensity, 1.24 g/cm³ by default).

Can it read .bgcode files?

No. Binary G-code is not read. Export plain text G-code from your slicer instead.

Does it work in Cursor or Windsurf?

Yes. Cursor, Windsurf and VSCodium install extensions from Open VSX. Search for the extension in their Extensions view and install it there.
