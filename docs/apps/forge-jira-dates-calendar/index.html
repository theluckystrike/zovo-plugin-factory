Issue Dates Calendar for Confluence

Put Jira issues on a calendar inside any Confluence page. You pick a JQL query and a date field, and the macro shows a month grid, a week or a day-by-day list. The date can be due date, created, updated, resolved or any date custom field, alone or as a start and end pair. Every issue links to Jira.

Each reader sees only the issues they can see in Jira, because the search runs with the reader's own Jira permissions.

What it does

  • Month view shows whole weeks, starting Monday or Sunday. Week view shows seven days, and List view shows the days of the month that have issues. Readers can switch views and move with Previous, Today and Next.
  • The date can come from Due date, Created, Updated, Resolved, or a custom field of type Date or Date Time (Jira's "Start date" is often customfield_10015).
  • Add an end field and an issue appears on every day from its start to its end. If one of the two dates is empty, the issue shows on the other date.
  • Month cells show the issue key, a short summary and an Overdue lozenge for unfinished issues whose due date has passed. Overdue appears only when the due date is the field being shown. Week cells add a status lozenge in the status category colour. List view and the day panel show the full row, with status, issue type, time of day for date-time fields and the span.
  • A month cell lists 3 issues and a "+N more" button (week cells list 15). The button opens the full list for that day under the calendar.

JQL errors show Jira's own message. A custom field that doesn't exist or isn't a date is named in the error. Cut-off results and issues without a readable date are reported under the calendar.

Setup

A site administrator installs the app for Confluence and then for Jira on the same site. Both installs are needed (see Requirements).

  1. Edit a Confluence page, type /Issue Dates Calendar and insert the macro.
  2. Enter the JQL query, for example project = ABC AND statusCategory != Done. It's the only required setting.
  3. Pick the date field, or pick "Custom date field" and enter its ID (customfield_10015, cf[10015] or 10015).
  4. Leave the end date field on "None" for one date per issue, or pick a second field for spans. Then set the default view, the first day of the week and an optional title.
  5. Publish the page.

To find a custom field ID, go to Settings, Issues, Custom fields in Jira and open the field's Edit details page. The number in the page address is the ID.

How dates are placed

Date fields, such as Due date, Start date and other Date custom fields, are calendar days and are never shifted.

Date-time fields (Created, Updated, Resolved and Date Time custom fields) land on the day they fall on in the reader's Atlassian time zone, which is shown under the calendar. Jira compares them in the reader's Jira time zone instead, and the two can be up to 26 hours apart. So the search is widened by two days on each side for date-time fields (one day for date-only fields) and then filtered by the local day. Issues near midnight aren't lost.

An issue whose end is before its start shows on its start day only, and the notice counts it.

JQL safety

Your JQL is wrapped as (your JQL) AND <date range> ORDER BY <date field> ASC, key ASC. Before that, the macro checks that parentheses and quotes are balanced outside quoted text and that no stray backslash could escape the added ). If something is off, it asks you to fix the query. Any ORDER BY in your query is ignored. The JQL goes only in the body of Jira's search API, never in a URL.

Permissions and data

  • read:jira-work lets the app search issues and read field names (to check custom date fields), as the reader.
  • storage:app holds the 5-minute cache of results and of custom field names and types.
  • There are no other scopes, no external servers (no egress) and no remote backend. See PRIVACY.md.

Requirements

Confluence Cloud and Jira Cloud must be on the same site. The macro calls that site's Jira as the reader.

The app must be installed for Jira as well as for Confluence. A site administrator adds it to Jira after the Confluence install. Without the Jira install, Forge refuses every Jira call with "The app is not installed on this instance", and the macro tells readers that an administrator needs to install the app for Jira.

In the manifest (app.compatibility), Confluence is the required product and Jira is optional. Forge's manifest check accepts only one required product, so Jira can't be marked required too. That's why the Jira install is a manual step. We haven't run the app on a live site yet. Public answers from Atlassian staff on the developer community say the second install is needed, so we treat it as required (see NOTES.md).

If Jira refuses a search for any other reason, the macro shows a permission message and says what an administrator should check. It doesn't show an empty calendar.
