Portal SLA Clocks for Jira Service Management

Portal SLA Clocks shows customers the SLA times on their own requests, right in the help center portal. They see how much time is left, when it's due, and whether a target was met or missed. Atlassian's request for this, JSDCLOUD-325, has 660 votes and has been open since 2014.

A project admin picks which SLAs customers see. Internal targets stay internal.

What customers see

The app adds a panel called "Service targets" to the request page in the portal. Each SLA a project admin picked gets one row with its name, a status lozenge and a short line of text.

StatusText on the portal
On track1h 30m left, Due by 6 Nov, 14:30 · Target 4h
Due soonSame as On track, once 25% or less of the target time is left
PausedPaused with 10m left, The clock is stopped for now · Target 4h
OverdueOverdue by 30m, Target 4h
MetMet in 45m, Target 1h
MissedMissed, took 26h 5m, Target 24h

The first part is the main line and the second is a smaller line under it.

Times come from Jira Service Management as they are. While an SLA is running, paused or overdue on a working-hours calendar, the row says "Counted in working hours", because 2 hours left might mean tomorrow morning. Met and missed rows don't carry that note, because Jira doesn't say whether a finished SLA ran on a calendar. Durations are shown in hours and minutes and are never turned into days, since a day means something different on every calendar. They're rounded down to the minute.

The due time is shown in the customer's own time zone. The panel says when it was loaded ("As of 14:30 your time") and has a Refresh button. It doesn't tick on its own, because a clock on a working-hours calendar stops outside those hours and the app can't know your calendar.

An SLA that hasn't started on a request (its start condition never matched) isn't shown. If a request was reopened, the panel shows the running cycle. Without one, it shows the result of the cycle that finished last.

Choosing what customers see

Open Project settings in a service project, then Portal SLA Clocks. Project admins and Jira admins can do these things there.

  • Pick up to 10 SLAs from the SLA fields on your site, or type a name. Names match without caring about upper case or extra spaces.
  • Give each one a friendlier name for customers, like "First reply" for "Time to first response" (up to 60 characters), and put them in the order customers should see them.
  • Decide what happens when an SLA is overdue or was missed. Show it, or hide that SLA from customers.
  • Turn off met and missed results on finished SLAs, or the due date and time on running ones.
  • Preview any request in the project with your unsaved changes, and see every SLA name on that request.

Until someone saves, the portal shows "Time to first response" and "Time to resolution", the two SLA names most Jira Service Management templates start with. If your SLAs have other names, nothing shows until you pick them.

If the app can't read the saved settings, customers see a message that SLA times aren't available right now, and no SLA times. It never falls back to the two defaults then, so an SLA you removed or hid can't reappear. The settings page shows an error and no Save button when the read fails. If the saved record is damaged, the page says so, shows the defaults, and lets you save over it.

Who can see the panel

People can see the panel only on requests they can already open in the portal.

For customers, the app asks Jira as that customer whether they can open the request. Jira only lets customers read SLAs if they're agents, so after that check the app reads the SLAs as itself and passes on only the SLAs you picked.

Some portal users have an Atlassian account but no customer account, for example Jira users who raise IT requests. Forge doesn't let apps act as those users. For them the app reads the request as itself and shows the panel only if that person reported the request or is listed as a participant. A request shared with them only through an organization shows "This request isn't available to your account" instead. Anonymous portal visitors don't get the panel at all, because Forge doesn't offer this module to them.

Permissions and data

The app runs on Atlassian Forge with no outside servers and no calls to other sites. It asks for three scopes.

ScopeWhy
read:servicedesk-requestCheck that a person can open the request, and find the service desk for a project
read:jira-workRead the SLAs on a request, check project admin rights, and list SLA fields on the settings page
storage:appKeep each service project's settings

It can't change anything in Jira. It stores one settings record per service project (SLA names, names for customers and three switches). It doesn't store SLA times, request data or anything about people. See PRIVACY.md for details.

Limits

  • Up to 10 SLAs shown per project, with names up to 255 characters and customer names up to 60.
  • Up to 4 pages of SLA records read per request, which is 200 SLAs when Jira sends 50 per page. If a request has more, the panel says some may be missing.
  • Up to 4 pages of participants (200 at 50 per page) checked for portal users without a customer account.
  • Each load stops calling Jira after 15 seconds and won't start a new call with less than 2 seconds left. Busy answers from Jira (429 and 503) are retried twice.

Install from source

You'll need Node.js 22 and the Forge CLI.

npm install
forge register        # replaces REPLACE_ME in manifest.yml with your app id
forge deploy -e development
forge install --product jira

Run the checks with npm test (unit tests), npm run validate (offline manifest check, plus a check that every module key exists in the Forge schema) and npm run check-frontend (UI Kit sources). The last two use the Forge CLI you have installed.
