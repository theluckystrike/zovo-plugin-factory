Privacy policy for Bulk Page Operations for Confluence

Last updated 2026-11-05

No data leaves Atlassian. The app has no servers of its own and no external permissions. It uses no analytics, cookies, CDNs or third parties, and everything runs on Atlassian's Forge platform.

What the app reads

The app reads Confluence data as you, with your own permissions. That covers page IDs, titles, ancestors, labels, the space you're in and the operations Confluence says you may perform there. It also reads the status of copy and archive tasks it started for you. Pages you can't see never come back to the app.

Forge gives the app your Atlassian account ID, the space key and the license state, like it does for every app.

What the app stores, and for how long

Storage is Forge app storage (KVS), which Atlassian hosts with your site's data.

Space admins, as confirmed by Confluence, see each job's operation, status and counts. Other users see only their own jobs. Page titles, the target page's title and failure details are shown only to the person who started the job.

The account ID of the person who started a job is personal data. It stays in Forge storage hosted by Atlassian and expires with the job record. Once a week the app reports the account IDs it holds to Atlassian with the Forge personal data reporting API. When Atlassian says an account is closed, the app removes that account ID from its job records (a record that would expire within a minute is deleted). The app stores no names, emails or other profile data, so nothing else needs updating.

What the vendor collects

Nothing. The vendor gets no personal data, page content, usage data or telemetry. The app writes standard Forge function logs with error names and codes, without page content.

Data subject requests

The vendor holds no data. Every record expires on its own after 30 days at most, and account IDs of closed accounts are removed within about a week of the weekly report. Uninstalling the app removes its access to your site.

Security

  • All Confluence calls use asUser(). The app never calls Confluence as itself.
  • Job batches run only for the person who started the job, from their own open page. There are no background steps.
  • CQL is wrapped so it can only match pages in the current space, and requests go only to a fixed list of Confluence REST paths.
