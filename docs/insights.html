Biggest insight (7 November 2026)

Rank wins, not product count. 64 products have brought 39 installs and $0. Installs come almost entirely from ranking in the top 3 for a phrase people search for. DOCX Viewer alone brings half of all installs, because it sits at #3 for "word viewer". More products will not change that. Rank in big-demand niches will.

Standing rule. For every product we keep, the goal is to be the best one on its platform for its main phrase: the features users ask rivals for, a first screen that shows them, real screenshots, a name that starts with the phrase, and ratings. A new product starts only when the re-measure shows we can reach the top 3 in a niche with real demand. On 7 November from 11:00 to 12:30 we did this for 8 listings: the AI coding tools, the table viewer, the DXF viewer, the video player and the NetCDF viewer. The re-measure at 07:30 on 8 November shows which of them climbed.

We rate this sprint 64 out of 100. Building and shipping went well, with 6 products on stores and 19 apps deployed on Atlassian. A browser test then found 10 of 19 Atlassian apps broken although review passed them. 16 of 19 pass now. Bulk Page Ops still fails (Confluence returns HTTP 410 to its label call), and 2 are not testable yet. Revenue is $0, and two store gates block it.

What we learned

  1. Rank wins, not product count. 64 products brought 39 installs and $0 (2026-11-07 12:30, 22 VS Code listings live). Installs come from top-3 rank on phrases with demand. DOCX Viewer brings half of all installs from #3 on "word viewer". Make each kept product the best on its platform for its main phrase before building new ones.
  2. Seven new listings and 33 updates did not move installs on the same day. Installs were 39 at 10:51 and still 39 at 12:30, while 7 new extensions went live and 33 updates shipped (F, public gallery). The search index needs a day or more to re-score a new name, so judge the releases on the 8 and 9 November re-measures, not today.
  3. Small niches give top 3 on the first day. Big phrases do not. NetCDF Viewer is top 3 on all 4 of its phrases, and the table viewer reached #2 on "xlsx diff" and "excel compare" right after its release. The big phrases (csv viewer, claude code usage, video player) belong to listings with thousands to millions of installs. A name alone does not pass them. Installs and ratings do.
  4. A check on real data still finds bugs after every test passes. A fresh lane ran the 4 AI-tool updates on the real Claude Code, Codex and Gemini folders of this Mac. It found 4 bugs that the unit tests and the release test missed. For example, Team plans showed no weekly limit. All 4 were fixed and published as 0.2.2 within the hour.
  5. Every product now points to the others. All 22 VS Code extensions, 9 Apify Actors and 43 site pages show GitHub Sponsors, the GitHub profile and 3 to 5 related Zovo products. PayPal needs only a link from the owner. One command adds it everywhere.
  6. Owner sign-ins are still the gate. Atlassian will not start reviewing the 15 submitted apps until the owner completes Partner Verification, which needs identity data. JetBrains waited 40 minutes for a sign-in that did not come, so Context Drift is not uploaded there. Open VSX is now with Eclipse staff (issue 15379). Apify, Shopify and Roblox wait for identity or account steps.
  7. Code review is not enough. Run the app. A browser test on 2026-11-06 found that 7 of 19 Atlassian apps crashed at start, although all 7 passed review with scores of 92 to 100. We fixed and redeployed them the same morning. Now no app can be marked ready without a runtime pass on its deployed version. 17 of 19 apps pass. Bulk Page Ops is fixed (version 1.1.1 reads labels through the v2 API, because Confluence removed the old one). Portal SLA and StatusBeacon need a Jira Service Management project to test.
  8. Store search is where Apify users come from, and we are not in it. For 10 of our 11 main keywords, none of our Actors is in the top 100 Store results. The top 10 for "youtube transcript" have 15,238 users in 30 days. Free channels such as Discord and Reddit bring about 7 paying users in total.
  9. The Atlassian apps hold most of the expected money. The base case is about $92,000 in 12 months for all 19 apps. The first 5 to list are AskSpace ($23,000), SignOff ($13,600), Markdown + Mermaid ($8,100), MathBlock ($7,900) and EpicTotals ($5,700). These are estimates from rival install rates, and the method is on the Money page.
  10. VS Code is the only store with real pull. Context Drift has 171 downloads with no promotion (highest of 6 readings, because Marketplace servers return different counts). On 2026-11-06 we added 5 file-viewer extensions. They are Skill Shelf, Tensor Viewer, HDF5 NetCDF Explorer, DOCX Viewer and PPTX Slide Viewer. 8 extensions are now live. Each has a public repo and a landing page on this site.
  11. The Apify numbers are almost all ours. Each Actor shows 2 users, which are our account and the Apify daily auto-test. All runs are our tests and fixes. Apify Store search takes days to rank new Actors, so a quiet first day is normal.
  12. The 19 Atlassian apps are the largest stock of finished work. They are built, reviewed and deployed. They earn nothing until the owner adds partner payment and tax details and we submit paid Marketplace listings.
  13. Owner steps are the bottleneck, not building. The sprints shipped 46 products. 4 owner steps block the money. They are the Open VSX Publisher Agreement, Apify business verification, the Atlassian Partner Agreement and verification, and Atlassian payment details. Free Atlassian listings do not need bank details.
  14. Free on Atlassian is permanent. If an app moves from free to paid, existing installs lock until an admin subscribes. Apps that run in Jira and Confluence must keep licensing, so Issue Dates Calendar stays paid. 7 apps are now free builds, to collect installs and reviews first.
  15. The VS Code Marketplace limits new extensions to 7 in 12 hours. Updates have no limit. The web upload form dropped uploads with no error, so for 5 hours no new extension went live. Trusted publishing through GitHub Actions fixed it on 2026-11-06. 7 new extensions went live in 15 minutes, and the next ones publish by script when a slot opens.
  16. The upload path was the bottleneck, not the products. For 5 hours no new VS Code extension went live, because the web upload form dropped files with no error. A lane found trusted publishing through GitHub Actions. After that, 7 new extensions and 24 updates were published in 2 hours with 0 failures, and the account holds no token.
  17. Installs come from search inside VS Code. DOCX Viewer got 11 installs from 4 web page views. The name decides the search rank, so lane A3 renamed 7 listings to start with the phrase people type, for example Claude Code History & Codex History Viewer.
  18. A test in the real app finds what unit tests miss. Real VS Code runs found product bugs in DICOM, ZIP, Arrow and SMV builds, and a real Minecraft server run found that BucketBackup left out the main world data. All were fixed before release.
  19. Modrinth is the one new store that needs no owner step. Agents made a token and submitted 2 mods. Async Locator 26 cut the longest server tick from 1,368 ms to 17 ms in a real server test.
  20. Building is fast. Store limits set the pace. Agents build and test a new viewer in 25 to 40 minutes, including a test in a real VS Code window. The queue holds 8 finished extensions and 3 more are being built. At about 7 new extensions each 12 hours, the queue is full until 2026-11-08.
  21. A test in real VS Code finds bugs that unit tests miss. The release test ran JSONL, SQLite and Agent Session viewers in a real VS Code window. It found and fixed 6 bugs. All unit tests had passed before.
  22. Search rank comes from the display name. In Marketplace search, the exact search words in the display name decide the rank group. Inside a group, shorter names that start with the words win, then installs. Keywords only break ties. We renamed all 8 live extensions to match this, for example "CSV Viewer, Excel Viewer & Parquet Viewer".
  23. Open VSX has real demand for the same viewers. On Open VSX (used by Cursor and Windsurf), one JSONL viewer has 24,478 downloads and one CSV extension has 361,835. We have 0 there. One script publishes all our extensions when the owner signs the Open VSX agreement.
  24. Ratings help search, so each extension now asks one time. All 15 of our extensions now ask for a rating one time, after 5 good uses over at least 2 days. The prompt never shows in the first session, and the user can turn it off. The extensions send no telemetry.
  25. The review loop worked. Each product went through independent reviews until it scored 92 to 100. Reviews caught an App Reviews run that could hang, a silent install failure in MathBlock, and a Secret Scanner setting that Atlassian does not allow.
  26. First builds always failed their first review. The median first score was 56. One fix round added 15 to 45 points. A reviewer on a different seat finds what the builder misses.
  27. Research prices were 3 to 4 times too high. We now price against the cheapest working rival, at $0.0001 per review, $0.001 per trends result and $0.0025 per transcript.
  28. A real platform run is part of the gate now. All 4 first Actors failed the Apify empty-input auto-test, and no reviewer caught it. Every prefill field now needs a default.
