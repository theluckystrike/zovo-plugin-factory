Insights and live numbers

Updated 2026-11-08 14:14 (UTC+7).

Biggest insight (8 November 2026)

Rank wins, not product count. The factory has $0 revenue. On 8 November at 07:30, 29 live VS Code listings had 102 installs (55 at 19:01 on 7 November). The 7 listings added at 22:57 brought 1 of the 47 new installs. Installs come almost entirely from ranking in the top 3 for a phrase people search for. DOCX Viewer brings 28 of the 102, from its rank on "word viewer" and "docx preview". More products will not change that. Rank in big-demand niches will.

Our standing rule: for every product we keep, the goal is to be the best one on its platform for its main phrase: the features users ask rivals for, a first screen that shows them, real screenshots, a name that has every word of the phrase, and ratings. Wave11 put this rule into the products that earn installs: DOCX, PPTX, NetCDF, Video, SQLite, Table, PDF and Tensor got their largest updates, each checked again in real VS Code by a second lane. A new product starts only when the re-measure shows we can reach the top 3 in a niche with real demand. The 07:36 releases are judged at about 15:00.

We rate wave11 (7 November 21:45 to 8 November 08:30) 57 out of 100. The products that earn installs got deep, red-teamed updates, the release window ran on time, and installs went from 55 to 102. Open VSX is verified. But revenue is $0, ratings are 0 on 29 listings, no money gate moved through our work, the 7 new listings brought 1 install, the effect of the releases is not measured yet, free disk fell to 6 GB at 23:15 and a network outage stopped 3 lanes for about 1.5 hours.

What we learned

  1. Wave12: no money path reaches $1,000 a month in 90 days. MONEY1 estimates $0 to $20 a month at 30 days and $20 to $300 at 90 days (E). Paid Apify Actors rank first: 10 owner minutes for the ID check, but a new Actor has a median of 1 user at 30 days. Atlassian paid apps rank second, with the first dollar at 4 to 9 months. VS Code donations bring about $0.01 a month at 136 installs. The only money so far is $195 in 11 one-time GitHub sponsorships, and 10 of them came before our first product (F).
  2. Wave12: installs come from search inside the editor, not from the listing page. DOCX Viewer had 22 installs on 15 page views in the publisher hub window (F, CONV1). In the search list a user sees the icon, the name, about 50 characters of description, the install count and the stars. The 5 best converters are top 3 on their main phrase against rivals with few ratings. The 5 worst sit outside the top 10 against rivals with 280,000 to 6.8 million installs. So ratings and rank decide, not README text.
  3. Wave12: 6 new niche listings went live, and the red team stopped 4 crashes first. PUB3 published Mermaid, Hex, SVG, GeoJSON/KML/GPX map, VCD waveform and PCAP from 12:23 to 13:49 (F). Before that, VER8 ran 8 builds in real VS Code 1.142 and 1.90.2 and found 4 crash-class defects: Find in the hex viewer froze the extension host, nested SVG use tags took the renderer past 8 GB, a 300 MB XLSX cell took the reader past 12 GB, and a zip bomb killed the DOCX web host. All 4 were fixed before publish.
  4. Wave12: Office files are used together. In 2,261 repos with Git LFS lists, 90 % of repos with .docx files also track PDF, 45 % spreadsheets and 36 % .pptx (F, XSELL1). Office files almost never meet tensor or NetCDF files. The File Viewers pack has 1 install, so it becomes an Office Viewer Pack with the 4 Office viewers only (E: about 180 installs in 30 days).
  5. Wave12: Open VSX works for Cursor and Windsurf, but its downloads are mostly bots. All 29 builds pass in a real VS Code 1.90.2, below Cursor (1.128) and Windsurf (1.141), so no editor is blocked (OVX1). We are top 3 on 29 of 60 Open VSX phrases. Open VSX downloads went from 1,892 to 4,308 in 90 minutes with no VS Code change, so we read them as an upper bound, not as users.
  6. Wave12: search engines and AI answers do not show us yet. None of 14 web and AI queries for our phrases showed a Zovo page (F, DIST1). The root robots.txt of the GitHub Pages host lists 9 sitemaps but not ours, and 28 of 29 repos have no homepage link. Obsidian submission by the owner is the best next channel per hour.
  7. Rank wins, not product count. On 8 November at 07:30, 29 live VS Code listings had 102 installs and the factory had $0 revenue (F, public gallery). The 7 listings added at 22:57 brought 1 install. The 22 older listings brought 46 overnight with no release. DOCX Viewer has 28 of the 102 installs from its top-3 rank on "word viewer" and "docx preview". Make each kept product the best on its platform for its main phrase before building new ones.
  8. Wave11: installs grew 85 % overnight with no release. Installs went from 55 at 19:01 on 7 November to 102 at 07:30 on 8 November (F). No listing had a release in that time, because of the 7-hour rule. Top-3 phrases held at 103 of 282 (102 at 18:16). So the gain came from the ranks that the 7 November renames won, and from the US day. Batch-1 listings made 5.9 installs a day each, batch 2 made 1.7 and batch 3 made 2.6 (F, re-measure).
  9. Wave11: more listings in small niches did not bring installs. The 7 extensions that went live at 22:57 (G-code, EPUB, ZIP, Font, HAR, Audio and the viewer pack) are top 3 on 4 of 41 phrases and have 1 install after 8 hours (F, RANK9). Their ranks did not change from 1 hour to 7 hours after release. A phrase ranks well when each of its words is in the display name, in any order: median rank 6 when all words are in the name, median rank 39 when one word is missing.
  10. Wave11: the install-earning products got their biggest updates. DOCX Viewer 0.3.0 (equations, charts, a 47 MB report in about 1.5 s), PPTX 0.4.0 (a 197 MB deck in under 5 s, SmartArt, slide to PNG), NetCDF 0.4.0 (0.25-degree grids and a 472 MB file now plot, coastlines), Video 0.2.5 (sound without ffmpeg), Tensor 0.4.0 (.pt and .pth files with a safe reader), plus fixes in SQLite, Table and PDF. 9 names gained a missing search word, for example "PPT Viewer", "MKV" and "SQLite DB Viewer". B-REL published 29 of 29 staged updates to the VS Code Marketplace from 07:36 to 08:17, one per listing, with 0 failed runs. The gallery showed all 29 new versions at 08:18 (F). Open VSX had the same version for 21 of 29 at 08:18, and a follower script publishes the rest.
  11. Wave11: a second check in real VS Code found 8 defects after all tests passed. Two red-team lanes ran 9 staged builds in real VS Code against the gallery builds, on more than 113 real files each. They found and fixed a PPTX zip bomb (webview memory 10 GB down to 146 MB), a tensor pickle loop, 9 unsafe pickle names, a text .pth regression, a SQLite view that never ended, a rating ask after an empty file, Windows-1252 CSV text, and 2 walkthroughs with text that failed the style check. 74 malicious pickle files ran 0 payloads.
  12. Wave11: Open VSX opened, and the money gates did not. Eclipse closed issue 15379 as granted at 03:54, and the zovo namespace is verified with 29 extensions (F). Cursor, Windsurf and VSCodium users can now find them. Revenue is still $0. Atlassian added an owner step at 03:36: a Stripe Identity check on ECOHELP-182590 by 6 December. JetBrains (4 plugins), Apify, Modrinth and Obsidian did not change.
  13. Wave11: ratings are still 0 on 29 listings. Crowded phrases (pdf viewer, sqlite viewer, excel viewer, claude code usage) belong to listings with 100,000 or more installs and many ratings. Rating ask v2 (3 good opens on 2 days, at most 2 asks, never after an error) is in the 8 updated builds. The #1 SQLite and Excel viewers get 1-star reviews about paywalls, so our listings lead with free, no ads and no account (COMP1).
  14. Wave10: a name that starts with the search phrase is the fastest lever. From 15:00 to 18:16 on 7 November, top-3 phrases went from 70 to 109 of 290 tracked phrases (55 at 07:35). Top-10 phrases went from 139 to 176. 13 releases after 15:00 (12 of them name changes) gave 29 new top-3 phrases in 1 hour or less, with 1 loss. Example: the JSON viewer went from 0 to 5 top-3 phrases, and "json tree viewer" went from #37 to #3.
  15. Wave10: installs followed the ranks. Installs went from 41 at 15:02 to 54 at 18:17 and 55 at 19:01 (F). That is 4.0 an hour, against 0.47 an hour from 10:45 to 15:02. 11 of the 13 new installs went to listings with a release that afternoon, for example the first 2 installs ever on the video player after "mov" reached the top 3. The time of day is a confounder, because the US morning starts then. The 8 November 07:30 re-measure is the test.
  16. Wave10: wait 7 hours before you judge a change. Some changes reach search rank in minutes. Others take 4 to 7 hours. 11 new top-3 phrases came on 4 listings with no release after 13:21 local time, for example "claude code history" from #56 to #1 on the session viewer and "dxf viewer" from #6 to #1. The 15:00 conclusion that keyword-only changes do not move rank was wrong. Do not release the same listing again within 7 hours.

The SQLite viewer got "Database Viewer" in its name and went from #13 to #4 on "database viewer", but "sqlite viewer" stayed at #10 and "db file" fell from #3 to #5. The PPTX viewer got no new top-3 phrase. "pdf viewer" went from #19 to #12, not top 3. "claude code usage" stays at #79, because #1 has 77,519 installs.
